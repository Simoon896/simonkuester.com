Earlier this week I was in Washington, D.C. for Identity Week America! It ran September 2nd and 3rd at the Walter E. Washington Convention Center, and I was there with Genix Cyber to demo Argus. Here's how it went, what we showed, and what I took away from it.

## The booth

We were at booth 728 with the full setup: banners, a demo screen, and a table covered in Argus swag. Most of my time went to walking people through Argus on that screen. Argus is our converged cybersecurity platform, which means threat detection and identity sit in one place instead of being spread across a pile of separate tools.

Explaining something out loud to a stranger in two minutes is a lot harder than building it. You find out fast which parts you actually understand!

## Showing off NHIAM

The part I was most excited to show was NHIAM, the non-human identity side of Argus that we've been building.

Here's the problem it goes after. AI agents need access to real systems to be useful, and a lot of the time they get it through a shared API key or a password pasted into a config file. Later on nobody can say which agent did what, and nobody remembers to take the access away.

NHIAM treats an agent like any other identity. Each one gets registered with an owner, and registering it doesn't give it access to anything. Access is a separate request that a person has to approve. The agent never holds the real credential, everything it does is logged under its own name, and if something looks off you can shut it down right away.

> Having an identity and having access are two different things.

That's my favorite moment in the demo. Right after an agent gets registered, it can't reach a single thing. Most setups blur those two steps together, and that's how you end up with an agent that can touch far more than anyone meant it to.

## Everything else going on

When I wasn't at the booth, I walked the expo floor and sat in on what I could. Identity Week covers a lot of ground: government ID, biometrics, digital wallets, fraud prevention. Most of the floor was about proving a person is who they say they are. We were there talking about identities that aren't people at all, so it was interesting to see where the two overlap.

## Seeing D.C.

I also got a little time to see the city. One evening I walked down to the National Mall and caught the sunset over the World War II Memorial, with the Lincoln Memorial off in the distance. Not a bad way to end a day on a convention floor.
